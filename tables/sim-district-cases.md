# Four case-study districts: same decline, different starting points

Cell: Reading G4. National 2019 SD = 38.5 score points.

Four hypothetical districts share the national shape of the 2024 score
distribution and differ only in where their median sits: -0.2, -0.1, +0.1, +0.2 national SD.
Each is asked to restore its OWN 2019 distribution, so every district
suffers the identical national decline. This holds the loss constant and
varies only position.

## 1. The requirement is identical everywhere. That is the point.

| District | Median offset | Median, points | D(p10) | D(p25) | g*(p10) | g*(p25) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| District A | -0.2 SD | 216.9 | -10.0 | -8.3 | 0.259 | 0.216 |
| District B | -0.1 SD | 220.8 | -10.0 | -8.3 | 0.259 | 0.216 |
| District C | +0.1 SD | 228.5 | -10.0 | -8.3 | 0.259 | 0.216 |
| District D | +0.2 SD | 232.3 | -10.0 | -8.3 | 0.259 | 0.216 |

By construction the decline, and therefore the restoration requirement, is
the same in every district. A district-level analysis that stopped here
would conclude that position does not matter.

## 2. But the same students sit at very different national percentiles

| District | Its p10 sits at national pctile | Its p25 sits at national pctile | Its median sits at |
|---|---|---|---|
| District A | 7.5 | 20.0 | 42.0 |
| District B | 8.7 | 22.4 | 45.9 |
| District C | 11.5 | 27.8 | 54.2 |
| District D | 13.1 | 30.9 | 58.4 |

This is where position bites. Even with medians only 0.4 SD apart end to
end, District A's own bottom decile sits near the national 7th percentile
while District D's sits near the national 14th -- twice as high. District
D's own bottom quartile sits above the national 30th percentile, so a rule
written on the national 25th reaches only part of it (Section 3 quantifies
how much).

## 3. What a nationally targeted program delivers in each district

Suppose a program is offered to students below the NATIONAL 25th percentile
-- a plausible eligibility rule -- and delivers the best-evidenced at-scale
tutoring effect of 0.155 SD to those it reaches.

| District | Share of its students eligible | Share of its p25 target covered |
|---|---|---|
| District A | 30.9% | 100% |
| District B | 27.8% | 100% |
| District C | 22.4% | 90% |
| District D | 20.0% | 80% |

A rule written on the national distribution is not a neutral instrument.
It sends systematically more resource per student to low-median districts
and less to high-median ones, regardless of how much each district lost.
With these modest offsets the effect is graded rather than dramatic, which
is itself the useful finding: a 0.4 SD spread in district medians already
produces a meaningful difference in who a national rule reaches.
Whether that is desirable depends on whether the goal is recovery
(restoring each district's own 2019 level) or equity (bringing every
student to a common national standard). The two goals are not the same,
and this table is the clearest way to show it.

## Caveats

- These districts are hypothetical. Real districts differ in the SHAPE of
  their distributions, not only in location, and they did not all suffer
  the national decline. Fahle et al. (2023) find districts differed sharply
  from one another in how much they lost.
- The offsets are deliberately modest. Real district medians span a far wider
  range, so the contrasts below are conservative.
- National percentiles outside the published 10th-90th range are
  extrapolated with a fitted normal and should be read as approximate.
