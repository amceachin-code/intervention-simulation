The State of Teacher Induction in Urban America
https://doi.org/10.1177/016146811211401410
abstract source: eric EJ991011

Teacher induction programs have been used by districts and schools to help respond to high turnover and inadequate preparation among beginning teachers. These programs are offered to novice teachers entering their own classrooms and are designed to provide professional development (PD) and support. Although most districts use some form of teacher induction or mentoring, the intensity, focus, and features of individual induction programs vary (Fideler & Haselkorn, 1999; Smith & Ingersoll, 2004). Services might include school and district orientation sessions, special in-service training and PD sessions, mentoring by an experienced teacher, classroom observation, and formative assessment (constructive feedback). By easing the transition into the classroom, induction programs are thought to have the potential to keep new teachers in the profession and improve their practice. The current study provides a comprehensive portrait of typical induction support provided to elementary school teachers in urban districts around the country during their first year. The authors' data present the perspectives of district leaders, principals, and beginning teachers, enabling them to describe the substantial services offered by districts and schools as well as services received by beginning teachers. The authors' analysis also provides information on the helpfulness and coordination of these services. (Contains 7 tables and 4 notes.)
